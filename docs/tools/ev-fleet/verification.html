EV Adaptation model Denmark: verification

Version 0.1 (BETA) · checks run on 28 November 2026

How the EV Adaptation model (Elbil-tælleren) is checked: automated tests, a monthly release gate, a symbolic check of the methods note, a backtest and an independent code review.

This page lists every check that has been run on the model behind Elbil-tælleren, with the results, including the failures and the open issues. Nothing here is a formal proof. The tests compare the code against independent oracles, the symbolic check recomputes the equations and numbers of the methods note, and the review is a second opinion by a different AI model.

Automated tests

The tests run with python -m pytest src in the development repository; the production subset of the code is published in tools/ev-fleet/pipeline/.

Area What is checked
Share model The Kalman-filter likelihoods equal a direct evaluation of the same multivariate normal density (an independent oracle); the logistic curve, the sampling variance, the December/January dummies, and that fuel shares sum to one
Market size Gompertz end points and its differential equation; the AR(1) likelihood against a direct multivariate normal; recovery of known Gompertz parameters from simulated data; the exit-rate identity
Stock-flow simulation The stock-flow identity holds every month and draw; negative sales are clipped without breaking it
Exits by fuel Conservation in the exit allocation; the share- and age-linked exit rules recover known slopes and saturate; the observed young share from BIL21 excludes the all-ages row; the age anchor is the year of the starting rate
Tax and CO2 module Per-car figures against a hand calculation; components add up; scenario quantiles are ordered; the turn-of-the-year case (2026 complete, no 2026 months in the forecast)
Export and data The month-over-month archive; observed headline inputs; earlier survival-model code

Monthly release gate

The model is refitted automatically when Statistics Denmark publishes a new month (GitHub Action). A fit is published only if every parameter has R-hat below 1.01, there are no divergent transitions, and the effective sample size exceeds 400. If a fetch, a fit or the export fails, nothing is committed and the previous forecast stays online.

Symbolic check of the methods note

Every labelled equation and numeric claim in the methods note was translated into SymPy and recomputed, with the numbers checked against the model output files (report, re-runnable script).

  • 138 claims checked, 136 passed. The identities of the Gompertz curve, the logistic share curve, the exit-rate rules and the stock-flow identity hold, and every cell of the backtest table matches the backtest output.
  • 2 failed, both corrected in the note. The note said that BEVs were 2% of the fleet at the December 2020 origin; the share was 1.2% (2% is the share a year later). It also gave 8–21% for how often the BEV scrappage floor binds; the current backtest gives 11–24%, and that number is now generated from the backtest output.
  • 19 claims are not machine-checkable, for example modelling assumptions and statements about the data sources.

The check is conditional on the translation from LaTeX to SymPy, which the report prints for every claim.

Backtest

At each origin the data are cut, the model is refitted, and the forecast is compared with the stock that was later observed. Error of the median BEV stock forecast:

Origin After 1 year After 2 years After 3 years
December 2020 −21% −9% −7%
December 2022 −1% −7% −12%
December 2024 −3%

The model beats a linear trend and a frozen-share baseline at every origin and horizon, but it still underpredicts the BEV stock. This is a retrospective comparison made during development, not an independent test: the exit rule, its 60-month window and the prior on the long-run BEV share were chosen after these results had been seen. The monthly archive of forecasts is the proper test, and it starts with version 0.1.

Independent code review

On 28 November 2026 the code, data, methods note and app were reviewed by Codex (OpenAI), with the instruction to find what is wrong or misleading.

# Finding Status
1 The tax figures rest on assumed tax rates (the 2020 average registration tax comes from a parliamentary answer that has not been verified against the source) Open: to be labelled as an illustration against an all-petrol comparison
2 The tax section would have disappeared in January 2027 Fixed, with a regression test
3 The exit rate and its age anchor referred to different dates Fixed: anchored at the year of the starting rate
4 The young-share proxy overstated the young fleet, because exported cars stayed in the count Fixed: observed ages from Statistics Denmark (BIL21)
5 The bands leave out monthly sales noise and the dependence between model layers Open
6 The backtest uses choices made with hindsight Addressed: stated in the methods note and on this page
7 Registration tax on used imports and export refunds is not included Open: the registration component covers new cars only, which should be stated
8 A revision of history without a new month does not trigger a refit, and a failed run leaves no visible warning Partly fixed: a failed tax calculation is now flagged on the page
9 A suppressed count in the source data could become a zero Open
10 A milestone that has been reached would keep moving into the future Open (relevant from about 2028)
11 Some labels state more than is computed (for example a fixed 15-year life in a label) Addressed in part

What is not checked

The model’s assumptions themselves (the long-run BEV share, the import rule, the link between the age of the fleet and its exit rate) are not verified by any of these checks; the methods note states them and the backtest tests their combined effect. The tax and CO2 figures are illustrations built on stated assumptions, which can be changed on the page.
